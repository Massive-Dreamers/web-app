# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Four audiences visit this site, and the design must serve all of them without splitting its voice:

- **Players** — arrive from a trailer, tweet, or Steam link. Goal: wishlist Mind Your Stay, follow future updates, feel the world before they play it.
- **Press / media** — journalists, streamers, and content creators looking for the press kit, high-res assets, quotes, and studio background for a piece.
- **Publishers / investors** — evaluating whether Massive Dreamers is a serious studio worth backing, publishing, or partnering with. They read between the lines: production values, clarity of vision, evidence of taste.
- **Potential hires** — developers, artists, writers considering the studio. They want to see how the studio thinks and what kind of work happens here.

The same editorial voice speaks to all four. Differentiation happens only through specific affordances (wishlist, press kit, contact, careers), not through separate tones.

## Product Purpose

The public face of Massive Dreamers — a Baku-based independent studio building narrative-first games. Flagship: **Mind Your Stay** (2027), a noir narrative RPG set in Hotel Safe Haven during a political revolution. Additional concepts (Project Echo Horizon, Project Chrono Archive) signal a slate, not a one-shot.

The site's job: introduce the studio, showcase the games, publish dev thinking (Newswire), and route each audience to what they came for. Success looks like Steam wishlists driven, press coverage secured, and industry conversations opened.

## Brand Personality

Considered. Cinematic. Uncompromising.

Voice is measured and confident, never breathless. References espionage, memory, moral consequence, dream-logic. It reads like a filmmaker's production company page, not a game marketing site. When it talks about the studio, it talks about craft and intent, not features and specs.

The atmosphere is noir and literary: charcoal night, warm cream paper, burnt orange like a low-lit sign. No exuberance, no hype cadence, no exclamation marks in copy.

## Anti-references

- **AAA marketing bloat** (EA / Ubisoft): popup barrage, hype-cadence copy, gigantic pre-order CTAs, every section a sales pitch. This site is the opposite of that energy.
- **Generic indie / itch.io** aesthetics: bare dark hero, monotone game grid, "wishlist on Steam" as the entire personality. We are indie, but the presentation is not.
- **Corporate SaaS**: white backgrounds, gradient hero text, hero-metric templates ("100% Independent" as a big number). Refuse the entire SaaS visual vocabulary even where it superficially fits.
- **Cutesy / cozy indie**: pastel palettes, pixel-cute mascots, wholesome tone. Mind Your Stay is about consequence in a hotel during a revolution. The site's mood must not undercut that.

## Design Principles

1. **Editorial over marketing.** Treat every page like a magazine spread with a considered opening, a rhythm, and a close. Not a landing funnel of stacked CTA sections.
2. **Restraint carries weight.** Silence, negative space, and slow reveals communicate seriousness. When everything shouts, nothing lands.
3. **One voice, four doors.** Players, press, investors, hires all read the same page. Differentiation happens through affordances (wishlist button, press kit link, contact form), never a change in tone.
4. **Atmospheric, never decorative.** Motion, color, and texture must serve the world of the games. If an effect doesn't reinforce the noir/dream-logic mood, it doesn't earn its place.
5. **Show craft, not claims.** Details prove seriousness far better than adjectives. Real dev writing, real screenshots, real names, real dates outrank "AAA-quality" or "revolutionary" every time.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG AA for text contrast and interactive target sizes across the site.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` — the site relies heavily on Framer Motion, so an alternate low-motion path must exist for hero and section entrances.
- Public marketing site: no known specialized user requirements, but keyboard navigation must work end-to-end (nav, filters, form, footer links), and focus states must be visible against the dark palette.
- Must render legibly on mobile (portrait phones) and desktop up to ~1440. Wider viewports are welcome to breathe with generous margins rather than stretch.
